## A garage with no wrench

Every other game in this issue lets you rebuild your machine. *Ring of Red* doesn't. GameSpot's 2001 review put it bluntly: "Walker designs can't be altered in any way." Hardcore Gaming 101's 2016 retrospective agreed that the infantry squads are "the only customizable part of the player's fighting force." It still belongs in a garage issue, because the squads you assign are a loadout with real consequences. They decide what a walker can do, how fast it reloads and how far it sees, and if you lose the walker you lose all three squads for good.

This article covers Konami's North American release, SLUS-20145, which shipped in mid-March 2001 after a Japanese launch in September 2000. Hardcore Gaming 101 traces the core staff to Sega's *Iron Storm* team, and credits *Panzer Dragoon* designer Yukio Futatsugi, who went uncredited, with world and battle-system design.

The setting is an alternate 1960s. Japan is partitioned after the war, and both halves field armoured fighting walkers (AFWs): heavy, slow machines closer to tanks on legs than to Armored Core's acrobats. The pace is slow by design, and every source below agrees on that.

## Three squads, one machine

Every AFW carries three infantry squads. One rides as **crew**: it loads the gun, supplies special shells and changes the walker's stats. The other two fight on the ground beside it. Jon "Zandar" Mott's 2001 GameFAQs guide, the most detailed numbers source consulted, sets out six squad types:

- **Infantry:** basic anti-personnel riflemen. "Terrible in Crew."
- **Shooter:** rocket teams, the best long- and medium-range threat to enemy walkers, with poor armour.
- **Recon:** deadly to infantry up close, and their Scouting ability starts your walker at its best range.
- **Medic:** best at healing soldiers, and surprisingly well armoured.
- **Supply:** "THE best Crew load numbers, period," and strong at repairing the walker during Recover.
- **Mechanic:** the best close-range anti-walker squad, and the only one that adds movement. Their repair skills fix damaged legs and guns.

Each squad can hold up to three abilities: a **frontline skill** that charges and fires from the vanguard (grenades, homing shots, snipers, mines, electrified wire), a **rear-guard skill** such as repair or a morale boost, and a limited stock of **special shells** for the crew (armour-piercing, HEAT, white phosphorus, illumination, smoke, incendiary) that don't recharge mid-mission.

The walkers come in four classes, each built for a range. Light AFWs reload quickly at short range. Standards fight at medium range behind an extra shield that can't be repaired mid-mission. Four-legged walkers hit hardest at long range but load slowly. Anti-AFWs are brawlers that can end a battle with a punch. And the square you attack from sets the opening range: front, side or rear is short, a diagonal is medium, and two or more squares away is long.

So the "garage" here is a matching problem: walker class, attack range and squad mix. Zandar's sample loadouts give a sense of it. A four-legged walker might carry Infantry, Shooter and a Supply crew for faster loading. He also wants an Illumination Shot in every unit, because night battles halve base accuracy.

## The ninety-second duel

You move on a square grid, with turn order driven by a clock rather than alternating sides. Moving, attacking and recovering each add time before a unit's next turn, and terrain both slows units and protects them. When two units meet, the game cuts to a real-time duel. Most sources give it a 90-second limit, although OPM's review said 60. You can only advance or retreat along a single line. The gun loads, and when you choose to fire at the enemy walker, a scope opens and your hit chance keeps climbing for as long as you hold. If you get hit while aiming, you lose the aim.

GameSpot summed up the whole game in one line: "The trick to Ring of Red is learning exactly how long is too long." Edge called it "a cerebral game of Chicken." GamePro's practical advice was to aim for 70 to 90 percent unless the clock is nearly out. You also move squads between the vanguard and the rear in real time, trading their attack power against your walker's exposure. Hits can disable the legs (half speed, then immobile) or the gun (half accuracy). The clock pauses while you're in menus.

## Acquisition and loss

There's no shop and no money. New squads arrive three ways. Every mission brings an issue of ordinary reinforcements, and Zandar observed that faster missions bring more. Better squads are freed by capturing towns, and you lose them if the enemy gets there first. A few exceptional squads are rewarded for alternate objectives. Dead squads are dead: RPGFan's Andrew DeMario wrote, "There're no Phoenix Downs." Lose an AFW and its pilot sits out until the next mission and gets no experience, while its squads are gone for good. Pilots level up and unlock Maximum Attacks, special moves usable a limited number of times per mission. Losing Masami, the protagonist, or running out of mission time ends the mission.

## The first session

The game's first two missions are training: a single walker against mock opponents, then a staged exercise that turns into the theft that drives the plot. Squads don't arrive until the third mission. IGN warned that these "dishwater-dull training missions … give an entirely incorrect impression," and Eurogamer agreed. GameSpy added that you won't get far if you skip them.

A plan built from Zandar's guide, GamePro's tips and the reviews:

- **Save at camp before every sortie.** START, then Stop, makes a suspend save mid-mission that's deleted when you load it. That's the feature that makes long missions manageable when you can't play for hours at a stretch.
- **Read the in-game Combat Manual.** Zandar says it explains soldier skills, crew shells and Maximum Attacks better than the printed manual.
- **Play the range game.** Light and anti-AFW walkers go in front, Standards in the middle, four-legs behind.
- **Shoot the walker, not the infantry,** unless the enemy squads carry wire, co-op or sniper abilities.
- **Don't put Masami on point.** The AI targets him whenever it can.
- **Grab recruit towns early** for the better squads.

## Who it suits, and what it costs

**Fit.** The decisions are excellent: a strict, readable rock-paper-scissors of range and crew, with permanent losses that make each squad you keep feel earned. It's the Front Mission and Carnage Heart audience that IGN recommended it to.

**Friction.** The pace is a real problem, and it's worth knowing the numbers before you start:

- **Missions are long.** Reviewers reported anywhere from twenty minutes to six hours. GameSpot counted ten to thirty skirmishes per mission at about four minutes each.
- **The campaign is long too.** HowLongToBeat users report around 49 hours for the main story.
- **Animations can't be skipped.** EGM's Mark MacDonald complained about "drawn-out attack animations you see hundreds of times but can't skip." GamePro's verdict: "hypnotically fun to some and sleep-inducing to others."
- **Text.** Between missions there's unvoiced reading. The US translation was widely criticised, and the US release cut references to the commanders' Nazi pasts, which Hardcore Gaming 101 says leaves Western players confused.

So, despite the suspend save, it is not a game for quick bursts.

## Controls and emulation: what's documented

Two GameFAQs guides describe the controls as a cursor plus a short command menu, with X opening commands and L1/R1 cycling units. They disagree about whether Circle or Square cancels, so check in-game. RPGFan's Alan Knight complained that you can't rearrange squads while aiming without losing the shot.

PCSX2's archived wiki rates the US disc Playable. One tester finished it at 4x internal resolution on a PCSX2 1.5 development build. The grid lines in cutscenes when upscaling, a known issue, are now handled by automatic fixes in PCSX2's current game database. Other PS2 emulators may not carry those fixes. Field Notes did not play-test the game for this issue.

## How it was received

Reviews from March to August 2001 split mostly on pace. Miguel Lopez gave it 7.9 at GameSpot and called it "easily the best strategy game out on the PS2 at this time." Edge gave it 8/10 and called it "An unexpected delight." The rest, grouped by how warm they were:

- **Higher:** IGN's David Smith 8.5, PSX Extreme 8.8, GameSpy/PlanetPS2 87%, and RPGFan's Alan Knight 90%.
- **Middle:** OPM 4 out of 5, Gamecritics 7.5/10, Eurogamer 7/10 for the PAL release, GamePro 3.5/5 for fun, and Game Revolution B−.
- **Lower:** RPGFan's Andrew DeMario 67%. In EGM, Ryan Lockhart's legible score was 5.5, and the "Worst Feature" line read "Gets mind-numbing a few hours in". The Gaming Intelligence Agency gave it 2 out of 5 for its "bowdlerized story and botched translation".

Metacritic's aggregate is 82 from 15 critics. Hardcore Gaming 101's 2016 retrospective found it plausible as a simulation but "too repetitive and slow" in its real-time battles.
