## Why this one opens the issue

Metal Gear Solid V is usually remembered as the series' big systemic sandbox, but most of its management loop first appeared five years earlier on a handheld. *Metal Gear Solid: Peace Walker* (PSP, 2010) already had the Fulton balloon, the offshore Mother Base, staff sorted into divisions, and weapons you research rather than find. Kojima Productions even carried the working title *Metal Gear Solid 5: Peace Walker* during planning.

That makes it the natural opener for an issue about deep games that survive short, interrupted sessions. It also comes with one habit that works against short play: long story missions with no checkpoints. The way to enjoy it in small pieces is to treat the story as something for a proper sitting and to treat Extra Ops as the everyday game.

## The loop, in plain terms

Peace Walker is split into two halves that keep feeding each other.

- **Missions.** You choose from a Mission Selector. *Main Ops* carry the plot and unlock in strict order. *Extra Ops* are optional, repeatable side jobs: target ranges, document grabs, time trials. GameSpot's reviewer put their length at just a few minutes each.
- **Mother Base.** Between missions you manage a growing private army on an abandoned ocean platform. Recruits go into combat, R&D, medical, intelligence or mess-hall teams. Hurt soldiers go to sickbay. Disgruntled ones go to the brig.

What ties them together is the Fulton Recovery System. Knock out an enemy soldier or find a prisoner, clip a balloon to him, and he becomes staff. His stats decide where he's useful. Your R&D team's level, together with GMP (the game's currency), decides which blueprints you can actually build. Once a weapon is developed it's mass-produced for the whole unit. So every mission is also a recruiting drive, and every recruit changes what you can bring on the next one.

There's more on top. *Outer Ops* sends squads of your soldiers and captured vehicles into computer-resolved battles. They earn money and combat bonuses, and they can lose people for good. Bosses are AI-driven war machines, and how you take one apart decides which parts and AI boards you salvage. That salvage builds Metal Gear ZEKE, MSF's own walking deterrent, and how far you develop ZEKE changes the difficulty of the final story mission.

The interesting decisions are small and constant:

- Kill or subdue? After each mission you're graded, and heavy killing or repeated detection costs you rank and *heroism*. Heroism also pulls in volunteers between missions.
- Who goes up the balloon? You can hold a few hundred soldiers, and a mediocre grunt takes up a slot that a specialist could fill.
- Which blueprint gets your limited GMP first: a better non-lethal weapon, or the gear for the mission that's stopping you?
- Do you send your best soldiers on Outer Ops for income, or keep them out of harm's way? Outer Ops casualties can be permanent.

The camouflage index from Snake Eater is back too. It reacts to how you move, where you are and how big your weapon is, but you can't change uniform mid-mission. So your loadout choice before deployment matters more than it did in MGS3.

## The first session

The game opens with its storytelling at full volume. Much of the plot is told through animated, graphic-novel cutscenes, and some include button prompts that can change mission outcomes or recruitment. GameSpot noted that you can pause or skip most of them, but not the interactive ones. Then you're in Costa Rica, reading patrols and deciding whether a guard is worth the balloon. The screenshot at the top of this article shows the posture you'll spend most of the game in.

It feels like the MGS4 control vocabulary of crouch-walk, over-the-shoulder aiming, CQC and holding up guards, scaled down to a handheld. GameSpot described Mother Base as the game's home screen between missions. You finish a short mission, come back, and find a list of new people who need jobs.

## Who it suits, and what it costs

**Fit.** For players who liked MGS V's base-building, this is the same idea with more scaffolding and smaller pieces. Grades reward replaying missions with zero kills or zero alerts, so a mission you've already cleared is still worth playing. Extra Ops are the right size for a short session, and every one of them feeds the base.

**Friction.** Two warnings, both from the 2010 reviews:

- GameSpot pointed out that many story missions chain four or five areas with no checkpoints, and called them a poor fit for bite-sized portable play. Save those for when you have an uninterrupted hour.
- The game has no difficulty settings, and some boss fights were built with co-op in mind. Solo players may need to replay Extra Ops to level up heavy weapons before a tough boss.

The story swings between comedy, conspiracy thriller and military melodrama. If MGS V's quieter tone is what drew you in, expect more theatre here.

## Controls and versions

The PSP had one analog nub, and Peace Walker was designed around that. By GameSpot's description of the default scheme, the face buttons handle camera and manual aim, and the D-pad handles menus and context actions. There are three control presets in total, modelled on Portable Ops, Guns of the Patriots and Capcom's Monster Hunter. GameSpot also warned that cycling weapons or gadgets doesn't pause the game, so Snake stands exposed while you browse.

The later HD Edition for PS3 and Xbox 360 added proper dual-analog controls. This article is about the PSP original. Emulators such as PPSSPP let you bind analog-stick directions to PSP buttons, which in principle allows a second-stick camera; Field Notes did not play-test that setup for this issue.

One more documented detail: on the PSP, voiced 3D cutscenes and voiced in-mission radio calls need an optional audio data install to the memory stick. It's worth deciding at the start whether you want it.

## A first-session plan

- **Pick a control preset in the first mission and stick with it.** If your thumbs know Monster Hunter's camera habits, try that one.
- **Clear the first Main Op, then switch to Extra Ops for twenty minutes.** That's the short-session rhythm. Learn it early.
- **Fulton at least five soldiers.** Then open the staff list and see where the game put them. Reassign by hand at least once so you learn what the stats mean.
- **Replay one Extra Op with no kills and no alerts.** Compare the grade with your first attempt. That gap is the whole game in miniature.
- **Leave the long story missions for a proper sitting,** when you're settled and in no hurry.

## How it was received

Peace Walker was very well received. Wikipedia's reception summary reports that *Weekly Famitsu* gave it a perfect 40/40 in its May 5, 2010 issue, making it the first PSP game to earn that score. IGN's Greg Miller gave it 9.5/10 on June 8, 2010. At GameSpot, Jane Douglas gave it 9/10 on June 11, 2010. She praised its production values and how neatly the base management feeds back into the stealth, and she criticised the occasionally fiddly controls and the long, checkpoint-free story missions. Metacritic lists a Metascore of 89 from 66 critic reviews.

Its commercial story outside Japan was weaker. Wikipedia records poor early Western retail sales, and total PSP sales of 1.99 million copies as of 2014.
