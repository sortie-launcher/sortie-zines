## Why this one: the jungle, with the camera finally in your hands

Between the first *Metal Gear Solid*'s corridors and the open bases of *Peace Walker* and *MGS V* sits *Metal Gear Solid 3: Snake Eater*, and the version to play is the 2005–06 re-release, *Subsistence*. It's the game in this issue with the most moving parts, and the one where buying the right edition matters most.

It earns its place here because it stretches all three words in the title. *Unseen*: a camouflage percentage you manage by changing clothes and lying in the right mud. *Unfair*: bosses you can beat by draining their stamina, outwaiting them or shooting them before the fight. *Unscripted*: food, injuries and close-quarters combat that turn a guard post into a small survival puzzle. *Peace Walker*'s camo index came from here. This is where it was a whole game.

## Which Subsistence, and what's on each disc

The 2004 *Snake Eater* used a fixed, high camera, and reviewers' main complaint was that you kept switching to first person just to see who was coming. *Subsistence* fixed that. Konami released it in Japan on 22 December 2005, North America on 14 March 2006 and Europe on 6 October 2006.

The disc set differs by region, which matters if you're buying a copy:

- **Disc 1, "Subsistence"**: the full Snake Eater campaign, plus a new third-person camera on the right stick. It's the default view. R3 switches back to the old overhead "Normal" view. The disc also adds more uniforms and face paints, and the European Extreme difficulty from the PAL release.
- **Disc 2, "Persistence"**: the MSX2 games *Metal Gear* (1987) and *Metal Gear 2: Solid Snake* (1990) with new English translations, Duel Mode against the bosses, Snake vs. Monkey stages, the comedy Secret Theater, and Metal Gear Online.
- **Disc 3, "Existence"**: the cutscenes edited into a film of roughly three and a half hours.

North America's standard release has two discs; the third came only in a Limited Edition that Wikipedia says went to pre-orders. Japan's standard release also had two, with three in the first print. **Every European and Australian copy has all three**, a gesture for the late release.

Two warnings. Disc 1 can't read original Snake Eater saves. And Metal Gear Online's official servers are gone: North America closed on 2 April 2007 per Konami's notice, and Wikipedia gives late 2006 for Japan and October 2007 for Europe. A fan revival exists; this issue didn't examine it. Treat the online mode as a museum.

## The loop, in plain terms

It's 1964. Snake parachutes into Soviet jungle with almost nothing, and the game wants you to live off the land.

- **Camouflage.** A percentage at the top right shows how visible you are; in the screenshot at the top of this article, Snake's Splitter face paint and Tiger Stripe uniform read 0 percent beside a stump. You set uniform and face paint separately in the Survival Viewer menu. The index drops sharply when you move and changes with stance, so the right pattern lying still in the right grass beats any gun.
- **Stamina and food.** When stamina drains, your health recovers slowly and your aim shakes. An empty stomach growls, and guards can hear it. Animals killed outright become meat that rots over time. Animals caught alive with a tranquilliser or mousetrap stay fresh, but you only have three cages. Rotten food makes Snake ill, but you can throw it for a guard to eat.
- **Cure.** Injuries persist until you treat them in a menu: splints for fractures, a suture kit for cuts, the knife to dig out bullets, a cigar to burn off leeches. Untreated wounds lower your maximum health.
- **CQC.** Unarmed or with a one-handed weapon you can throw a guard, grab him, interrogate him, use him as a shield, drag him, choke him or cut his throat. Which one you get depends on how hard you press the button and which way you push the stick.

Bosses have a health gauge and a stamina gauge, and a fight ends when either is empty. Non-lethal wins pay: draining the sniper The End's stamina earns his rifle. And the game makes the "unfair" options legitimate. You can shoot The End earlier in the story, before his fight. Or you can save, put the game down for a week (or move the console clock forward), and find he has died of old age. Later, The Sorrow walks you upriver past the ghosts of everyone you killed, so the size of that crowd depends on how you played.

At the end you get a codename based on alerts, kills, saves, continues, time and healing items. Fan documentation says the top rank wants Extreme difficulty with no alerts, kills or continues. Treat that as a long-term goal, not a first playthrough.

## The first session

The first hour is the "Virtuous Mission", a prologue that doubles as a tutorial: a HALO jump that costs you your backpack, radio lessons on stamina and suppressors, a crocodile swamp, a camouflage lesson, a rope bridge where shooting a hornet nest scatters the guards, a rescue, and a betrayal that breaks Snake's arm and teaches you to treat injuries. Then the title sequence rolls and the real operation begins. Expect plenty of radio chatter and long cutscenes early. GameSpot and Eurogamer both put the campaign at about 15 hours.

## Fit and friction

**Fit.** If you liked *MGS V*'s sandbox or *Peace Walker*'s loadouts, Snake Eater is the ancestor of both: handmade areas rather than an open map, but enough interacting systems to solve a guard post with food, noise, mud, a dart or a throw. Six difficulty settings run up to European Extreme, where being spotted ends the game.

**Friction.**

- **Story density.** This is Kojima at full length. The cutscenes are excellent, but a short session can disappear into them.
- **Saving.** You save by radio almost anywhere, but the manual explains that loading puts you back at the last Continue Point, usually the last area transition. Save just after crossing into a new area.
- **Menus.** GameSpy's reviewer found constantly opening sub-screens for cures and camouflage "a drag", and called the controls an "unorthodox use" of the PS2 pad.

## Controls and emulation: what's known, what isn't

On a DualShock 2, the left stick moves and the right stick turns the camera. L1 aims and re-centres, R1 holds first person, L2 and R2 open items and weapons, Triangle is action, X crouches, Circle is CQC and Square fires. Two of those buttons are **pressure-sensitive**: the manual explains that releasing Square slowly lowers your weapon without firing, and that a hard press of Circle on a held guard cuts his throat while a light tap chokes him.

That's the main problem away from a real DualShock 2. The PCSX2 wiki says the game needs analog Square and Circle; one tester tuned Circle's sensitivity to stop killing guards he meant to choke. Most modern pads, and Android's gamepad input, report face buttons as plain on/off presses. **Inference, not tested:** without a pressure-modifier binding, every press arrives at full strength, so you'll fire whenever you raise your gun and may kill guards you meant to choke. Before committing to a playthrough in an emulator, check whether it lets you bind a light-press modifier.

PCSX2's database gives the US discs its top compatibility rating and applies automatic fixes for depth-of-field blur and missing menu text. The wiki also calls the game CPU-intensive, so lower-powered hardware may struggle. **Nothing here was play-tested for this issue.** Test the jungle foliage first and a CQC grab second.

## A first-session plan

- **Buy the European three-disc set if you want everything**, or the standard North American one if you only want the campaign and the MSX games.
- **Start on Normal.** Use the new camera. Switch to the overhead view with R3 once just to see what the original was like.
- **During the Virtuous Mission, open the camouflage menu at every new terrain type.** Watch the percentage before you move.
- **Catch one snake or frog alive and keep it.** It won't spoil.
- **Save at area transitions, not mid-crawl.**
- **Leave The End for a session when you have time to spare**, or plan to kill him early. Both are legitimate.

## How it was received

The 2004 original was already acclaimed: Metacritic lists 91, and Wikipedia reports IGN at 9.6, GameSpot at 8.7 and Edge at 8/10. Subsistence scored higher, mostly because of the camera:

- **IGN**, Jeff Haynes, 10 March 2006: 9.8, Editors' Choice. He wrote that the camera rejuvenates the game and that Subsistence "goes a long way towards remedying the few problems" of the original.
- **GameSpot**, Greg Kasavin: 9. Praised the camera and online mode, criticised the thin manual. The archived page carries a 2007 date but appears to date from the 2006 launch.
- **GameSpy**, Benjamin Turner, 16 March 2006: Editors' Choice. Called Duel Mode and the Secret Theater fluff; praised the MSX ports.
- **Eurogamer**, Rob Fahey, 24 October 2006, PAL three-disc set: 9/10. The definitive version, he said, but poor value if you already owned Snake Eater.

Metacritic's normalised average for Subsistence is 94 from 53 reviews. Wikipedia reports that IGN ranked it third in its 2009 list of the top 25 PS2 games.
