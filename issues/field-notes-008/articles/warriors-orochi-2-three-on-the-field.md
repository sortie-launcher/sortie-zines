## Two series, one forgettable plot

Warriors Orochi 2 reached North American PlayStation 2 owners on September 23, 2008. It is Omega Force's second crossover between Dynasty Warriors and Samurai Warriors, and the setup is unchanged: a serpent king has merged third-century China with sixteenth-century Japan. There are five stories: Shu, Wei, Wu, the samurai, and an Orochi prequel to the first game. Worthplaying counted eight missions in each.

You can ignore the plot. GameSpot's Carolyn Petit called each story "equally awful", with static portraits and clumsy dialogue. IGN's Ryan Clements noted there is no Japanese voice option. The interesting decisions are what you build between battles and how you use the two characters who aren't on screen.

## The bench is a resource

You take three characters into battle and switch between them with L2 or R2. The US manual's key rule is that characters who are resting slowly refill their Life and Musou gauges. A wounded Speed character can rest while a Power character holds a gate, and then come back healthy.

There are limits. You can't switch while you're being hit. If the character you're controlling falls, the battle is lost, even if the other two are fine. EXP and level-ups only go to the character on screen. Worthplaying found that whoever kills fastest ends up ten levels ahead of the others.

Characters in reserve still help in the fight. If a waiting character's Musou gauge is full, L1 with Square or Triangle calls them in for a Support Attack while you're under attack. During a Musou attack (Circle), L2 or R2 chains in a teammate whose gauge is also full. With your health in the red and a full gauge, L2 and R2 together trigger a Triple Attack with all three characters.

## Power, Speed, Technique

Every character belongs to one of three types, and R1 does something different for each.

- **Power** characters spend Musou on a Power Attack. Their Hyper Armor means normal attacks and arrows don't knock them back mid-swing.
- **Speed** characters have two Combination Maneuvers. Pressing X in the air sends them dashing forward, immune to attacks, and jumping cancels their attack animations.
- **Technique** characters can use R1 in place of Triangle to turn a charge attack into an Enhanced Strike. They score critical hits on enemies in the air, and R1 while being hit spends Musou on a Counter Strike.

The type mix decides what your Musou Chain adds. Two Power characters add Multi, which creates doubles. Two Speed characters add Agility, two Technique characters add Drain, and one of each adds Flash, which breaks guard.

## The garage: weapons, fusion and abilities

Between battles you plan at the Camp. Each treasure box you open gives every team member a weapon once the battle ends. A character can hold eight weapons, so a ninth means throwing one away. The manual lists fourteen effects, including Flame, Ice, Slay (more one-hit kills) and Rage (stronger the lower your health).

Fusion is where you make real trade-offs. You give up one weapon to add its effect to another, limited by the base weapon's empty slots. Fusion costs Growth Points, the same currency you spend on character levels, and spent points can't be refunded. Every fusion is a level some new recruit doesn't get. Treasures found in leather bags turn a weapon into an Upgrade Skill. You can attach up to three of these to one character's weapon, such as Revival (one full refill when your health hits zero).

![The manual's Customize Weapon page (page 22): Upgrade Skills, Weapon Fusion and the Growth Point cost. Warriors Orochi 2 PS2 manual, © 2008 Koei, scan via gamesdatabase.org](media/warriors-orochi-2-three-on-the-field-manual-weapons.webp)

Abilities are a separate layer. You unlock them by meeting conditions that are listed in Team Info. Up to seven can be equipped, and they apply to the whole team. If another character unlocks an ability you already have, it levels up. The Karma ability and the Hard or Chaos difficulties improve weapon drops. Each character's Proficiency gauge fills as they kill enemies and reduces the damage they take.

## Modes on the US disc

- **Story** has the five stories. A second player can join at "2P PRESS START"; GameSpot describes split-screen.
- **Free** replays any cleared scenario with any unlocked character. The manual recommends it for grinding.
- **Dream** has scenarios with fixed teams of characters who never normally meet; Wikipedia counts 28 stages.
- **Survival** is an endless run of three-on-three tag fights in an arena. Characters and weapons are preset, and wins pay Growth Points.
- **VS** is for two players and has four types: Tag Team, Elimination, Tower and a horse race, Steeplechase.
- **Gallery** holds profiles, movies and the Treasure Guide.

In battle, moving close to an allied force gives you a rendezvous, which regenerates health. Moving close to a third-party army forms an alliance, which also raises morale, and doing both multiplies the effect. Each character can also trigger one Strategy per battle, such as Shield or Volley, if they meet its requirements.

## A first session

- Start Story on Normal.
- At Camp, field one Power, one Speed and one Technique character to get Flash on your Musou Chain.
- Switch characters before your active one runs low. Losing them loses the battle.
- Check each character's Strategy and Ability conditions in Team Info.
- Pick up every treasure box and leather bag.
- Afterwards, decide whether this battle's Growth Points go to levels or to a fusion.
- Try one Survival run. Its characters are preset, so it's playable straight away.
- Stop at a results-screen save. Interim saves are erased once you clear the scenario.

## Fit and friction

The appeal is the planning: team types and shared abilities shape the squad, while levelling recruits and fusing weapons compete for the same Growth Points. The friction is the battles you are planning for. Petit described enemies who "wait calmly" to be killed, and allied captains who need protecting. Survival is the closest thing to a fighting game here. Worthplaying compared it to SoulCalibur, while Clements called the one-on-one Versus mode "laughable" and reported endless combos. VS and co-op need a second controller on the same console, and GameZone missed online play.

Battles are timed (the manual shows a 60:00 clock), so each one makes a natural session. The long haul is levelling 92 characters.

## Editions and controls

Nothing here was playtested. This article follows the US PS2 release (Redump: SLUS 21803). The upstream PCSX2 desktop GameIndex lists SLUS-21803 with no compatibility rating or fixes, and rates the PAL disc 5; neither figure is a tested result for the US disc.

The manual's control layout: Square normal attack, Triangle charge attack, Circle Musou (hold to fill the gauge), X jump, R1 type special, L1 guard, L2/R2 switch, R3 map, Select calls your horse.

GameSpot found the Xbox 360 version sharper. IGN reported slowdown in its split-screen mode and said a patch had been announced. The 2009 Western PSP port added Japanese voices, Memory Stick installation and characters from the Japan-only Musou Orochi Z.

## Reception in 2008

PS2 critics were harsh. Clements (IGN) gave it 4.5 and Petit (GameSpot) 4.0. Angelina Sandoval (GameZone) gave 5.5 but liked Survival and Dream. Matt Olsen (Worthplaying) gave 6.5 and said it was no more than "a rental". Metacritic averages nine PS2 reviews at 52. Wikipedia reports 946,131 copies sold in Japan by July 2008. Clements's 2009 review of the PSP port scored 5.2. This research found no substantial later reassessment.
