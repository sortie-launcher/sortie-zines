## Two discs, one roster

This article covers two PS2 discs: Dynasty Warriors 5 (US, late March 2005) and its expansion, Dynasty Warriors 5: Xtreme Legends (US, November 2005). Neither was played for this issue. DW5 holds the officer campaigns. Xtreme Legends holds the more unusual systems: a create-a-soldier career and a run-based survival mode with a shop, a blacksmith and no automatic health recovery between fights. Neither menu offers a tutorial; the manuals teach.

## The DW5 battlefield

The loop is one officer against a map-sized battle. You cut through hundreds of soldiers to reach named officers, and each officer you kill raises your army's morale. According to the US manual, killing a base's Guard Captain stops that base sending out fresh enemy troops, and losing bases lowers the enemy's morale. So you keep deciding whether to rush the commander or first shut the gates that keep refilling the field.

Inside fights, the decisions are these:

- **Weapons.** Each officer carries up to four weapons, each with a power rating, hit count, attributes and a weight. Light weapons are fast but weak; heavy ones hit hard but leave you open.
- **The Musou gauge.** A full gauge pays for a Musou Attack. On a weapon with the Evolution attribute, a full gauge instead extends the normal combo from six hits to nine without draining it. So you choose between spending the gauge and keeping it full.
- **Combos as loot.** The manual says power-ups dropped by officers and Guard Captains get stronger when you kill them during a long combo.
- **One bodyguard.** DW5 swapped the old squad of guards for one bodyguard who levels up. You can order it to attack, guard or hold position. When you stand close together with full gauges, the two of you can fire a Double Musou.
- **Musou Tokens.** A token dropped by enemies or crates lets you trigger Musou Rage on R3: you get faster and stronger, and you can use the True Musou Attack even at full health. Normally it needs a red life bar.

One-on-one officer duels, a DW4 feature, are gone. Kongming's Archives lists them as removed, while weapon deadlocks remain.

## Controls, per the manual

![The US Dynasty Warriors 5 manual's Controls spread, pages 4–5: Square normal attack, Triangle charge, Circle Musou, X jump and mount, L1 guard, R1 bow, R3 Musou Rage, SELECT bodyguard orders. NTSC-U manual scan, © Koei, via Internet Archive](media/dw5-xtreme-legends-officer-runs-manual-controls.webp)

The US manual lists Square for normal attacks, Triangle for charge attacks, Circle for Musou (holding Circle charges an unfilled gauge), X to jump and to mount or dismount, and L1 to guard. Pressing Triangle while guarding counters, and holding L1 while moving strafes. R1 raises the bow, R3 triggers Musou Rage and SELECT changes the bodyguard's orders. The game runs only in analog mode, so a controller without a clickable right stick needs R3 mapped elsewhere.

## Musou Mode and what needs the DW5 disc

Musou Mode is only on the DW5 disc. It covers 48 officers, six of them new. Wikipedia counts five stages per campaign (four for faction leaders and unaffiliated officers), each ending in that officer's own ending. Free Mode replays any stage you have cleared in Musou Mode, and both modes allow two-player co-op. How many times you can save mid-stage depends on difficulty: unlimited on Easy, three on Normal, one on Hard.

Eurogamer's review confirms that Xtreme Legends runs on its own. Legend, Destiny, Xtreme, Challenge and Edit modes need no DW5 disc. The Xtreme Legends manual also states the limit: DW5 is "required in order to play Musou Mode/Free Mode." The discs connect in two separate ways:

- **Load Original** reads the DW5 disc's scenarios into memory, after which you put the Xtreme Legends disc back in. The manual warns that a reset or power-off loses that data, so you repeat the swap every session.
- **Save import** happens only when Xtreme Legends first creates its save. A DW5 save on the memory card in slot 1 carries over officers and items. If an Xtreme Legends save already exists, the two cannot be combined.

Both links assume real discs and a memory card. Under emulation, Load Original means swapping disc images mid-session and the import means having a DW5 save on the virtual card in slot 1; neither was tried for this issue, and this article offers no workaround.

## Legend and Destiny

Legend Mode offers 18 single-stage scenarios with set officers; once you clear one, any officer can play it. Orbs and unique weapons drop only on Hard or above.

Destiny Mode is the new idea. You create a soldier and serve under an officer you choose. Your weapon decides your moveset, and you start with a two-hit combo. Before each battle you pick personal objectives, such as a kill count within a time limit, and completing them earns Skill Points. Those buy charge and Musou attacks and skills such as a fire raid on a base. Promotions give you troops to order with SELECT, and staying near your commander earns healing items. Your side may ask you to fake a defection; if you miss the window to turn back, you stay with the enemy. After eight stages the soldier can become an Edit officer. Destiny has no two-player option.

## Xtreme Mode is the run

![Xtreme Mode's Blacksmith screen at Mission 3: a Twin Blade (Power 3, 4 attacks, Medium) with upgrades priced in iron. English PS2 build from GameSpot's November 2005 review gallery; region not verifiable from pixels. © Koei, GameSpot watermark](media/dw5-xtreme-legends-officer-runs-xtreme-blacksmith.webp)

This is the roguelike in the package. You start at default stats and weapon level, and your health does not recover between missions. Points earned in battle convert to gold at the Rate of the destination you picked; a higher Rate means a harder mission and more gold. Gold buys healing, stat boosters and invitations to recruit officers, and shop prices rise with each purchase. Iron from officers and crates pays the Blacksmith for power, weight and up to five attributes. You can keep six allies and take three into battle; an ally who falls is captured and lost. The world setup is random. When the run ends you lose everything except 10 percent of your iron, which carries into the next run as Bonus Iron.

The manual also describes a kingdom layer. Every fifth mission is an important battle, a Unification score decides whether you keep territory, and escorted craftsmen build facilities that pay gold or iron. This is not Empires. There is no separate strategy turn; territory changes only through which of three destinations you fight next, and whether you win.

## A first session

- Start with Xtreme Legends' Xtreme Mode, which needs no DW5 data. Choose a low Rate and one of the three scenarios available from the start.
- My suggestion: at each Blacksmith stop, spend iron on weapon power before adding attributes, and keep enough gold for a Meat Bun.
- When the run ends, note the Bonus Iron it banks.
- Then play one Destiny stage with an easy objective to see how the skill tree begins.
- Leave DW5's Musou Mode for another evening; GameSpot said each story often takes "a couple of hours."

## Fit and friction

The standout is Xtreme Mode, where damage carries over, prices escalate and allies can be lost for good; if you like runs with lasting consequences, start there. Destiny is slower: IGN's Chris Roper found its starting moveset so thin that early battles were nothing but the Square button. The friction is the series itself: Eurogamer complained about a camera you keep re-centring with L1, and enemies often mill about. Legend and Xtreme modes support co-op on a horizontally split screen; in Xtreme, player two controls an ally.

The upstream PCSX2 GameIndex rates both US serials, SLUS-21153 and SLUS-21299, compatibility 5, and gives Xtreme Legends a hardware fix for "sun luminosity." That is desktop data, not a tested result.

## Reception, 2005–06

In 2005, GameSpot's Alex Navarro gave DW5 6.8 and called it "tired", missing DW4 Empires' strategy layer. IGN's Ed Lewis gave it 7.7, praising the single bodyguard and reduced fog. Xtreme Legends scored lower: 6.3 from Navarro, who singled out Destiny as the best addition, and 5.2 from Roper at IGN. In March 2006, reviewing the PAL release, Eurogamer's Tony Horgan gave it 6/10 and called it a low-risk introduction to the series. We found no later reassessment worth citing.
