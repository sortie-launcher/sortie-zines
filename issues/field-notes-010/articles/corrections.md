We checked our own work. Before this issue went out, every published Field Notes article, 001 to 009, went through a fact-check against the sources it cited and against better ones where they existed: review scores and dates, release facts, quotations, credits and the mechanics we described. About 890 claims held up. Forty-seven did not. They are all listed below.

We don't rewrite published issues. An article is a record of what we said and when we said it, so errors are corrected here, in public, and the original text stays as it was. Most of these are small. A few are the kind a reader should have been able to trust us not to make: a review score from a fan repost, a quotation with its halves swapped, a compliment credited to the wrong critic. The pattern is worth naming. Almost every serious error came from leaning on a secondhand copy of a source when the original was findable.

Items the audit could not prove either way are not listed. A correction should be as well sourced as the claim it replaces.

## The serious ones

- **006, Culdcept.** We gave Christian Nutt's GameSpy review 4/5 and said its date wasn't shown. The original GameSpy page, dated December 11, 2003, gave it 4.5/5 and an Editors' Choice. Our 4/5 came from a fan-site repost.
- **001, Carnage Heart EXA.** We said Digitally Downloaded's April 8, 2013 review published no numeric score. It rated the game 3.5 out of 5 stars.
- **002, Gungriffon Blaze.** We said it arrived "weeks after" the PlayStation 2. It was a North American launch title, on sale with the console on October 26, 2000.
- **002, Gungriffon Blaze.** Our Gamezilla quotation put its two clauses in the wrong order and changed a word inside the quotation marks. The review actually reads: "Repeated failures can quickly leave you at a disadvantage since the components selected are taken from your reserve whether you finish the mission or not."
- **007, Devil May Cry 3.** We credited the example of opening a combo with a sword, switching to a shotgun and finishing with another melee weapon to Greg Kasavin's GameSpot review. It comes from Miguel Lopez's GameSpy review (March 1, 2005).
- **007, Devil May Cry 3.** We said both Kasavin and Eurogamer's Kristan Reed criticised the backtracking. Only Kasavin did, as did IGN's Jeremy Dunham in his Special Edition review. Reed's complaints were the difficulty, the repetitive structure and the camera.
- **007, Devil May Cry 3.** We wrote that Capcom producer Matt Walker said DMC3 set out most of the genre's rules, including the end-of-mission grade. In the 2020 interview he said DMC3 set the course for the *series*, through styles and weapon switching tied to the Style Rank. He made no genre-wide claim and didn't mention mission grades.
- **003, Baroque.** We called the English patch for the PlayStation version "Plissken's translation". Romhacking.net credits the translation to the nervetower.neocities.org team; Plissken did the hacking and released the patch.
- **003, Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Explorers of Sky.** We said IGN called it "a rip off" for owners of Time or Darkness. Jack DeVries didn't recommend it even to newcomers and called it "a rip off, plain and simple".
- **004, Deception III.** We said "EGM's reviewers" called the translation "appalling". One reviewer did; a second criticised its "awkward English sentences".
- **005, God Hand.** We said Skull Cards raise how many Roulette Orbs you can hold. Per the US manual (p.17), they refill orbs: a Green Skull gives one, a Gold Skull two.
- **005, God Hand.** We credited the casino's chihuahua races to the manual. The manual lists slots, poker and blackjack; the races come from Wikipedia and Eurogamer's review.
- **008, Dynasty Warriors: Strikeforce.** We wrote that Destructoid named R as the air-dash button. Destructoid only says "a shoulder button"; IGN's review names R.
- **003, PMD: Explorers of the Spirit.** We said SkyTemple's directory lists the hack's removal of traps, monster houses and item loss. Those details come from the team's Project Pokémon release thread.

## Smaller fixes

### 001
- **Battle Garegga.** An extra life every 1,000,000 points applies only with the board set to the Japanese region. Other regions, including MAME's default (Europe), give no score extends unless the operator sets "every 2,000,000".
- **Battle Garegga.** Push Square's praise of Rev.2016 was narrower than we said: Damien McFerran said the option to fix rank per stage solves "one of the big issues raised by the game's small number of critics", not that the additions as a whole did.
- **Battle Garegga.** Our list of what raises rank left one out: destroying enemy bullets also raises it (cancelling them with a bomb doesn't).
- **Peace Walker.** Recruiting soldiers into teams began in Metal Gear Solid: Portable Ops (2006), not Peace Walker. Peace Walker added the Fulton balloon and Mother Base.
- **Peace Walker.** GameSpot said every cutscene can be paused and only some interactive segments can't be skipped, not that interactive cutscenes can't be paused or skipped.

### 002
- **Front Mission 3.** Not every wanzer part carries a skill: body, arm and leg parts do; backpacks and weapons don't.

### 003
- **Baroque.** Romhacking.net dates patch version 1.0.2 to 8 August 2022, not 7 August.
- **Baroque.** Sting developed the Saturn (1998) and PlayStation (1999) versions; Entertainment Software Publishing published them. Sting self-published only the 2007 PS2 remake.
- **Baroque.** Our RPGFan citation for the PS2 version linked to the Wii review. The PS2 review is from 9 April 2008, with the same points and the same 78%.
- **PMD: Explorers of Sky.** Metacritic's positive reviews are GameDaily's 100 and Nintendo Power's 80; it counts ONM UK's 70 as mixed, not warm.
- **Azure Dreams.** RPGFan's Andrew DeMario called it "the worst translation I have ever seen", stronger than "among the worst", and RPGFan gave the translation no separate score.

### 004
- **Deception III.** Game Informer's 6.25 review ran in issue #84, April 2000, not May.
- **Deception III.** Redump entry 18445 is the US disc (SLUS-01067), not the Japanese one, which is entry 27485.
- **Deception III.** PCSX2's database rates only US discs 1 and 2 as perfect, and its text fix is for the key-item pickup box, not "missing menu text".

### 005
- **God Hand.** 1UP's "simply asked if you were a bad enough dude" is about *Bad Dudes* specifically, which Andrew Fitch compared to God Hand's opening, not old brawlers in general.
- **Soldier Blade.** In the 2012 interview, only the asteroid stage was cut for schedule and memory. Ukiuki Uribo blamed the non-homing missile pods and the easy Bay Wolf ships on miscommunication in his own design documents.
- **Armed Police Batrider.** Only the four Battle Garegga guest ships use the "Garegga" shot type; the Mahou Daisakusen guests use the standard ones.
- **Alien Soldier.** Sega Retro's review range runs from 20 (Cool Gamer, Russia) to 94, not from the 40s.

### 006
- **Culdcept.** Land chains form only among same-element squares within the same map area.
- **Monster Rancher 2.** Style, not Loyalty, is the balance of Fear and Spoil; Loyalty is a separate stat that Fear and Spoil together determine.
- **Steambot Chronicles.** Metacritic's scores don't bottom out at 1UP's 80: it lists 35 reviews, 19 mixed and one negative. 1UP's wording was "occasionally janky controls".

### 007
- **Strider 2.** The North American discs are mislabelled: the disc printed "Strider 2" (SLUS-01142) holds the original Strider, and the one printed "Strider" (SLUS-01163) holds Strider 2.
- **Character action history.** Wikipedia attributes the borrowed concepts to beat 'em ups and fighting games together, not fighting games alone.
- **Blood Will Tell.** All seven chakras add luck, not most of them, per the GameFAQs table we cited.
- **Zone of the Enders: The 2nd Runner.** Digital Foundry's HD Collection figures (about 30fps, drops below 20) describe the PS3 version. It measured the Xbox 360 version 10–15fps faster.
- **God of War: Ghost of Sparta.** Our list of PPSSPP's built-in fixes for the US disc left out NullPageValid, which targets the Nexus of Atlantis crash. The manual workaround we gave may be unnecessary on builds with that flag.

### 008
- **Dynasty Warriors 5: Xtreme Legends.** Bonus Iron is 10 percent only when you leave via EXIT, and 5 percent if you're defeated.
- **Musou history.** *Shin Sangoku Musou* started a new Japanese numbering; Western releases kept counting from the 1997 fighter. That's why every later Western number is one higher, not because of the Japanese title.
- **Musou history.** Capcom's "so easy to play" describes why Sengoku Basara appealed to its female fans, not a design goal. The only design choice Capcom reports is leaving out blood.

### 009
- **Windjammers.** The FAQ author recommends Biaggi for beginners, prefers him to Costa, endorses Miller/Yoo too, and disputes only Mita's "beginner" label.
- **Windjammers.** A set at 12 points continues until one player leads by more than two; the 30-second clock still applies.
- **Tony Hawk's Underground 2.** Neversoft removed the game's message-of-the-day files after a July 2007 FTP hack, which effectively shut the official servers, rather than "pulling the server files".
- **SSX 3.** Redump has one disc entry for both editions, listing serials SLUS 20772 and SLUS-20772GH, which share the internal serial SLUS-20772.
