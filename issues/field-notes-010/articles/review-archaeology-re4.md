In January 2005 the big reviews praised Resident Evil 4's difficulty in nearly the same words, and none of them described the hidden rank that was producing much of what they praised. That wasn't carelessness. A single playthrough can't see a system built out of that playthrough. Reviews measure feel, and RE4's feel was partly the reviewer's own performance being fed back to them.

## What the reviews said

GameSpot's Greg Kasavin (10 January, 9.6) called it "a level of difficulty that feels just right." He added that the difficulty "scales up seemingly in perfect harmony with your growing comfort level." GameSpy's Ryan O'Donnell (6 January, 5 out of 5) wrote that play was "balanced enough to keep the game difficult without becoming frustrating." IGN's Matt Casamassina (7 January, 9.8) praised villagers who "team up, dodge bullets." That's notable, because how often enemies dodge is one of the things the rank turns up.

The magazines followed. EGM issue 189 (March 2005) scored it 10, 9.5 and 10 and made it Game of the Month. Its complaints were about strafing and a weak final boss, not balance. Edge gave it 9 out of 10 in its March 2005 issue and raised that to a 10 in its 2013 "Ten Amendments" feature. In Japan, Famitsu's four reviewers gave 10, 9, 9 and 10, a total of 38 out of 40. The PS2 port arrived that October and got the same treatment, including 9.5 from IGN's Fran Mirabella III.

Even Capcom talked about difficulty without mentioning the rank. In a 2005 Nintendo Dream interview, producer Hiroyuki Kobayashi said Shinji Mikami "did a great job fine-tuning the difficulty for today's gaming audience."

## When the dial surfaced

The information was in print within months, just not in reviews. The BradyGames *Official Strategy Guide* (2005) included one cautious sentence: "At the start of the Island stage, the game assesses the player's inventory and automatically adjusts the difficulty level to compensate for the possession or lack of powerful weapons." It was offered as a warning that item locations might not match. Famitsu's Japanese guidebook *Biohazard 4 Kaitai Shinsho*, also 2005, went further. The Resident Evil Wiki cites its pages 24–25 for full rank tables: how many shots enemies take and how much damage Leon takes, at the start and at the extremes.

Wikipedia credits a Future Press guide dated 4 November 2005 with describing a "Difficulty Scale" from 1 to 10. Its figures (Normal starting at grade 4 and moving between 2 and 7) don't match later measurements, so treat that one as unconfirmed.

For English-speaking players, the system went mainstream much later. Game Maker's Toolkit's "What Capcom Didn't Tell You About Resident Evil 4" went up on YouTube on 2 June 2015 and has passed two million views. In April 2016 the speedrunner Skullbot posted point values for individual actions on speedrun.com. GameFAQs testers published damage multipliers for each rank in 2023. That makes a decade between the reviews and the popular explanation. The licensed guidebooks are the closest thing to an official explanation, and beyond them, any confident claim about who "knew" in 2005 is guesswork.

## Feel is real evidence, and not enough

The critics' sentences hold up. Kasavin's "perfect harmony with your growing comfort level" is close to a working description of the rank. What went wrong was the attribution. The harmony was credited to authored level design. Part of it was a feedback loop reading his hits and misses, and no one-pass review could have told the difference. That's because the review copy was, in a small way, the reviewer's own game.

That's the lesson worth taking away. For any game that might adapt, a review's verdict on "balance" is testimony about one run. It is not a measurement of the design. In 2005 the reviews and the strategy guides were on the same shelf. One recorded how the game felt and the other recorded how it worked. Readers only asked the first one whether the game was fair. The best review of RE4's difficulty was written in the guide aisle.
