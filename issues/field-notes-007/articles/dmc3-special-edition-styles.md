## Why this one is here

Nothing here was played for the article. Devil May Cry 3 is in this issue because it set out most of the rules the genre still uses: a fighting style you choose, weapons you swap in the middle of a combo, a meter that grades every fight, and a separate grade at the end of each mission. Capcom producer Matt Walker said as much in a 2020 USgamer interview. The first Devil May Cry introduced juggles and Devil Trigger. DMC3, directed by Hideaki Itsuno, added switchable styles. It also made swapping weapons mid-combo routine, which Walker says sets the pace together with the Style Rank.

The version to play is Special Edition: North America, January 24, 2006, sold at a $20 budget price. That is also the version later included in the HD Collection and released on Switch.

## Four styles, four weapons

The PS2 layout is simple. Triangle swings the melee weapon, Square fires the gun, X jumps, R1 locks on. Circle is the style button, and what it does depends on which style you picked. Dante carries two melee weapons and two guns. R2 swaps the melee weapon and L2 swaps the gun, instantly. Greg Kasavin's GameSpot review explains why this matters: you can open with a sword, blast with a shotgun partway through, and finish with another melee weapon.

You choose one of four starting styles at the start of a mission or at certain checkpoints partway through:

- **Trickster**: evasion. At the start, Circle is an invincible dash in any direction.
- **Swordmaster**: extra moves for the melee weapons.
- **Gunslinger**: extra firearm techniques.
- **Royal Guard**: repels attacks with a button press and stores the energy for a counter.

Two more styles unlock later. **Quicksilver** slows enemies while Dante keeps moving at normal speed. **Doppelganger** creates a shadow double, and a second player can control it by pressing Start on controller two. Each of the four starting styles gains experience as you use it and unlocks new abilities as it levels, which is why GameSpot advised sticking with one style at a time.

![Style selection menu in the 2005 PS2 release: Trickster, Swordmaster, Gunslinger and Royalguard, with Gunslinger at level 1. © Capcom, screenshot via GameSpot's 2005 review](media/dmc3-special-edition-styles-style-menu.webp)

The deeper layer is cancelling. Dahbomb's long-running GameFAQs mechanics guide describes jumping or rolling out of a move's recovery, and kicking off enemies' bodies to stay in the air. Players learn to do it naturally because it's the main way to keep a combo going safely. You don't need it to finish the game. It's what keeps the game interesting on later runs.

## Meters, ranks and Devil Trigger

A style gauge rates each fight as it happens: Dope, Crazy, Blast, Alright, Sweet, SShowtime, and at the top SSStylish. It climbs when you vary your attacks without getting hit. It falls when you take damage, and it resets if it was at Crazy or lower. At the end of a mission you get a separate letter grade from D upward, based on clear time, red orbs collected, stylish play, items used and damage taken.

Red orbs are the currency. They buy new moves, gun upgrades and items. Blue orbs increase maximum health. Devil Trigger turns Dante into his demon form for a short time, making him faster and stronger and regenerating his health. Dante only gets it about a third of the way through the game. GameSpot named it as the point where the difficulty becomes manageable.

## What Special Edition changed

The original North American release was notoriously hard. Its Normal mode was the Japanese version's unlockable Hard mode. Continuing after death cost yellow orbs, which were expensive. IGN's Jeremy Dunham noted that the Japanese release had used a checkpoint system instead. Special Edition re-tuned the game:

- Normal now equals the Easy mode of the original North American release. "Very Hard" is that release's Hard mode under a new name. An Easy mode unlocks after a few deaths.
- An optional **Gold** mode lets you restart from a nearby checkpoint as often as you like. If you're carrying a gold orb, it revives you on the spot. The old yellow-orb system is still available.
- **Vergil** is playable, with one style (Dark Slayer), the katana Yamato, Beowulf, Force Edge, summoned swords, and Devil Trigger from the start. His run reuses Dante's missions without the story cutscenes.
- Also new: **Bloody Palace**, a 9,999-floor survival mode where portals let you skip 1, 10 or 100 floors and progress can't be saved; a new boss, Jester; a 20% faster **Turbo** mode; and a cutscene viewer.

Vergil and Bloody Palace unlock only after you finish the game once or load a save from the original release. Dunham thought Vergil should have been available from the start.

## Changing styles mid-fight on Switch

The Switch version, released digitally on February 20, 2020, adds Free Style mode. You switch between the four main styles mid-fight by pressing directions on the D-pad. It also adds local co-op for Dante and Vergil in Bloody Palace. In the same USgamer interview, Walker relayed Itsuno's reasoning: PS2 hardware made on-the-fly switching difficult "to an extent", but the restriction was mostly deliberate, because Itsuno felt players needed to be eased into the system. On Switch, Classic Mode keeps the original rules and Free Style is optional.

## The first session

Missions are short, and dying usually means replaying one mission, not losing an evening. A plan:

- Start a new game as Dante on Normal and choose the Gold continue mode.
- Take Trickster. GameSpot found that new players who charged in with Swordmaster or Gunslinger got "murdered repeatedly". The dash is your safety net.
- Practise the locked-on roll: hold R1, push left or right relative to Dante, and press X. Get the direction wrong and he jumps instead of rolling.
- You start with only the Rebellion sword and the Ebony & Ivory pistols. Once you've picked up and equipped a second melee weapon or gun, use R2 or L2 to swap mid-fight.
- Spend early red orbs on moves for the weapons you actually use, and keep a gold orb in reserve once you can afford one.
- Ignore mission grades and leave Turbo off. Clear two or three missions and stop.

## Fit and friction

The pull: this is a God Hand neighbour (Field Notes No. 005) with a build system. Styles, weapon loadouts and orb spending let you shape Dante, which reviewers compared to RPG-style character building. Every mission ends with a grade.

The friction is all on record. Kasavin called the original "unreasonably difficult at first" and noted it gives no invincibility after being hit. Eurogamer's Kristan Reed farmed secret missions for orbs and was stuck on one mission for a long time. Both reviewers criticised backtracking, and IGN still noted camera trouble in narrow corridors in SE. The tone is loud: Reed found the opening one-liners "cringe-worthy". Kasavin rated the cutscene direction among the best he'd seen. My judgement, not a sourced fact: SE on Normal, Gold mode, with Trickster, is the version for anyone who quits games that take hours to get good. The original yellow-orb Hard mode is for later.

## Playing it today

Special Edition is still sold in two forms: the HD Collection (PS3/360 2012, then PS4, Xbox One and Steam in 2018; Steam's store data still listed it for sale on October 24, 2026) and the Switch release. GOG added the HD Collection as a DRM-free release in June 2025. For the original PS2 disc, PCSX2's desktop game database lists the US Special Edition (SLUS-21361) as compatibility 5, with a rounding fix for a door in Mission 18. That is upstream desktop data, not a tested result.

## Reception, 2005 and 2006

The original was praised but criticised for its difficulty. IGN's Jeremy Dunham gave it 9.6 (February 25, 2005). GameSpot's Greg Kasavin gave it 8.6 (February 28). GameSpy's Miguel Lopez gave it 4/5 (March 1), comparing it to Ninja Gaiden. Eurogamer's Kristan Reed gave it 8/10 (April 29) and said Capcom had over-compensated with the hard default. For Special Edition (both reviews January 24, 2006), Dunham gave 9.0 and IGN made it Game of the Month. Kasavin gave 8.2, arguing that the easier game was "somewhat less rewarding". On Metacritic, an aggregator, the original averages 84 and SE 87.
