## Why it belongs here

If Devil May Cry and Ninja Gaiden are the Japanese trunk of character action, God of War is the main Western branch. It kept the combo strings, launchers and parries but changed the priorities: crowd control over duels, spectacle over precision, and an upgrade economy that makes you stronger whether or not you improve. Ghost of Sparta, Ready at Dawn's second PSP entry, is the series at its most compressed.

This article covers the US UMD release (serial UCUS-98737), released on 2 November 2010; Redump lists the US disc as version 2.00. The PS2 original and Chains of Olympus come up throughout as points of comparison.

## The Western branch of the tree

The 2005 PS2 manual sets out the template. Square and triangle are light and heavy attacks, circle grabs, L1 blocks and parries "if attack is blocked at right time", and the right stick evades. Red orbs fill a Power Up meter that you spend on the Blades of Chaos and on magic. Rage of the Gods is triggered by clicking both sticks. There are four difficulty settings, Mortal, Hero, Spartan and God, and God unlocks only after you finish the game once. Progress is saved at Save Altars.

The chained blades sweep wide, so a crowd is the normal fight. Grab finishers with button prompts turn big kills into a reward, and orb upgrades carry an average player forward. Simon Parkin's Eurogamer review puts it fairly: button-mashers on low settings get "one of gaming's most compelling power fantasies", while players who learn dodge and parry timing find a game that "rewards practice".

## What you actually do

Chains of Olympus had already moved the roll from the right stick to both shoulder buttons plus the nub. GameSpot's Aaron Thomas called that "a bit of an adjustment". The WingedMasamune GameFAQs guide gives Ghost of Sparta's PSP layout: L blocks, a tapped L deflects, and L+R plus the nub evades. Magic sits on d-pad up, left and right, and d-pad down swaps weapons. L plus circle performs the Hyperion Charge, a tackle. This appears to be the "run-and-tackle move" Game Informer's Joe Juba called new.

Levels alternate fights with block or body puzzles, climbing, swimming and chest rooms. Green and blue chests refill health and magic, and red chests pay for upgrades. Gorgon Eyes, Phoenix Feathers and Minotaur Horns lengthen the health, magic and fire meters. The guide prices Blades of Athena levels two to four at 2,950, 8,550 and 17,500 orbs. Parkin calls the progression "prescribed" but says it "undeniably works".

## Thera's Bane and the Arms of Sparta

Ready at Dawn added a fire resource, a spear-and-shield weapon and new magic, all designed around the PSP's limited buttons.

- **Thera's Bane.** After you free the Titan Thera, holding R sets the blades on fire. IGN's Nicole Tanner notes that it has its own meter, which refills over time. Juba adds that some strikes plant delayed-blast explosives. You need it to break certain doors and armour, so you keep using it. 1UP's Chris Pereira found he attacked hard while the meter was full and evaded while it refilled.
- **Arms of Sparta.** A spear you can throw or jab with, and a shield you can slam into enemies or walk behind while blocking. IGN notes that the shield also gets you through fire and freezing wind.
- **Magic.** The Eye of Atlantis fires lightning, the Scourge of Erinys pulls enemies into a vortex, and the Horn of Boreas freezes them. The guide notes that the Eye and the Horn give brief invulnerability.

There is a cost: because R is taken by Thera's Bane, Pereira found magic awkward to reach while moving.

## A portable session

GameSpot's Tom Mc Shea says the adventure runs "more than 10 hours", and Sony advertised "over 25%" more gameplay than Chains, which GameSpot finished in under seven. The guide marks a save point in 27 of its 39 area sections. IGN found load times "practically non-existent".

Finishing the game unlocks the extras. The Challenge of the Gods has five Challenges of Ares and eight Challenges of Athena. The Combat Arena lets you set the backdrop, difficulty, enemy types and counts, and turn on infinite health. In the Temple of Zeus you spend orbs on art, videos and costumes. There is also a God Mode difficulty.

## First-session plan

- Start on the default difficulty. Mc Shea found parry timing annoying rather than punishing there.
- Use the Scylla opening to practise block, deflect and the L+R roll. The guide notes that its attacks can be blocked or deflected.
- Learn one combo. The guide's fallback is square, square, triangle.
- Spend early orbs on the blades, not spread thin across spells.
- Once Thera's Bane is unlocked, keep it running against armoured enemies and let the meter refill between groups.
- Stop at a save point rather than assuming a combat checkpoint has saved the session. The walkthrough lists frequent opportunities to save.

## Fit and friction

My judgement, not a review finding: this gets to the point faster than most of the genre. Within minutes of a storm at sea you are fighting the sea monster Scylla, and the systems are taught during the fight. It is not God Hand (Field Notes No. 005), though. These are authored set pieces on a forgiving curve, and Parkin called the water sections "unwelcome".

The friction is consistent across reviews. Juba found the shoulder roll "clumsy and unreliable". Mc Shea found Kratos easy to lose with the camera pulled back, and called mashing the shoulder buttons to escape certain attacks "downright painful". He also found the bosses "exceptionally easy". IGN credits a fix over Chains: button prompts now appear on the side of the screen that matches the button's position.

## Emulation notes

Nothing here was playtested. What follows is upstream PPSSPP data.

- PPSSPP's compat.ini turns on several fixes for UCUS-98737: buffered rendering, a forced default CPU clock, a fixed-60fps "GoW framerate hack", and the rotated-analog-stick fix first added for Daxter. The framerate hack also fixed issue #8299, where enemies spawned endlessly in The Vortex (closed 2022).
- Issue #14958 is still open: a crash when dropping down in the Nexus of Atlantis. Developer Unknown W. Brackets' workaround for the JIT is to turn off Fast Memory or keep "ignore bad memory access" on. Later commenters confirm it works.
- The public report page for this serial mixes Perfect, Playable, Ingame and Doesn't Boot results, mostly from budget phones, so it says little about any particular device.
- Reviewers found the L+R roll clumsy even on a real PSP. Henrik Rydgård notes that PPSSPP can bind one host input to two PSP buttons, so a single button could trigger L+R. This is untested.

## Reception in 2010

IGN's Nicole Tanner gave it 9.5 on 25 October 2010 and called it the best-looking PSP game so far. Game Informer's Joe Juba gave 9.5 the same day ("a must-play for God of War fans"). Joystiq's Randy Nelson gave 4.5/5 but warned that anyone "hoping for something really innovative" was "out of luck". 1UP's Chris Pereira gave an A-minus. GameSpot's Mc Shea gave 8 on 29 October. Eurogamer's Parkin was cooler at 7/10, calling it a step back after God of War III. Metacritic's aggregate is 86. The 2011 PS3 Origins Collection remaster is a later port.
