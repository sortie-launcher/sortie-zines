## Why Bayonetta belongs here

Bayonetta is Hideki Kamiya reworking the template he set with Devil May Cry. PlatinumGames developed it and Sega published it on Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3, in Japan on 29 October 2009 and in the West in January 2010.

Field Notes No. 005 covered God Hand, and the two are close relatives. Platinum was founded by former Clover Studio staff, and both games grade how you answer enemy attacks. God Hand's level meter shifts the fight as you play. Bayonetta keeps each fight fixed and awards a medal at the end.

## From Devil May Cry to Team Little Angels

Game Informer noted that Kamiya "created Devil May Cry while working at Capcom". He went on to direct Viewtiful Joe and Okami. At Platinum his team called itself Team Little Angels, a nod to DMC's Team Little Devils. He told GameSpot in 2009 that he never played DMC1 during development and played "about half of DMC4" for research. He also said most of the team had worked on DMC, and that "if there hadn't have been DMC, there wouldn't be Bayonetta". Eurogamer's Simon Parkin wrote that Kamiya had "stripped away all of the dead weight" of his own formula.

## Witch Time and the dodge offset

Everything starts with the dodge. The right trigger evades in any direction. The manual says a dodge "at the last possible moment" triggers Witch Time, which slows everything except Bayonetta, leaves nearby enemies defenceless and speeds up the magic gauge. Edge described the cues you react to: chimes "that build to a fine pitch" and a glint on the incoming weapon. According to the official BradyGames guide, Witch Time is easier to trigger on the easy modes and harder on Hard. On Non-Stop Infinite Climax, dodging never triggers it at all.

The dodge offset adds a second layer. Hold an attack button and dodge in the middle of a combo, and the manual says you "offset the current combo and continue it after the dodge". So you can sidestep a hit and still land the Wicked Weave at the end of the string.

## Weapons, weaves and the halo economy

Bayonetta equips weapons on her hands and feet separately, in two sets that the left trigger swaps even mid-combo. She starts with the Scarborough Fair pistols on all four limbs. Kamiya told GameSpot that some weapons fit both kinds of slot and others fit only one. The demon smith Rodin turns Angelic Hymn records you find into new weapons: shotguns, a katana, a whip, claws. Wicked Weaves are combo finishers in which her hair forms a giant fist or heel. Torture Attacks (punch and kick together, paid for with magic) summon guillotines and iron maidens.

Halos are the currency. At Rodin's Gates of Hell they buy lollipops, accessories and techniques, and you can try a technique before you buy it. One technique is Bat Within: press evade immediately after taking a hit and you burst into bats, which the guide says mitigates the damage. On Easy and Very Easy the game automatically equips the Immortal Marionette, the accessory behind its Automatic assist. Kamiya called Automatic "a helping hand from an incredibly skilled expert": you press one button, and the CPU positions Bayonetta and picks combos. The Climax Brace turns every attack into a giant summon. The guide lists it for sale after you collect all 101 Umbran Tears of Blood.

## Verses, medals and Alfheim

There are 16 chapters between the prologue and the epilogue, each split into verses, the individual fights. The guide's tables rate each verse on combo points, time and damage taken, each running from Bronze to Platinum. GameSpot and Eurogamer also describe a higher "Pure Platinum" grade for a flawless verse. The verse results add up to a chapter trophy, and the manual says its lowest rung is Stone. The results screen lists the items you used, and using none earns a bonus. Only Normal or harder posts to leaderboards.

![Gameplay HUD: health, magic, verse, halos, combo multiplier. © Sega / PlatinumGames; supplied with GameSpot's Xbox 360 review, capture platform not independently verified.](media/bayonetta-witch-time-hud.webp)

Alfheim portals are hidden challenge rooms with strict rules: kill only during Witch Time, use a fixed number of punches and kicks, or land a quota of Torture Attacks. Each rewards a piece of a Witch Heart (vitality) or a Moon Pearl (magic). Clear them all on Normal or above to open Angel Slayer, a gauntlet the guide numbers to 51 stages that unlocks a new character.

## A first-session plan

Nothing here was playtested. The plan is drawn from the manual, the guide and reviews.

- Start on Normal. The easy modes hand fighting to Automatic, and Alfheim portals don't appear below Normal.
- Use the loading screens. They're playable, and pressing Back turns them into a practice room with a move list. Eurogamer called this "ingenious".
- Drill two things, in order: the last-moment dodge, then the dodge offset.
- Expect Stone trophies early on. Nintendo Life warns you'll "pick up a bunch of stone awards". Check the verse results to see what cost you points.
- Spend halos on techniques before lollipops.

## Fit and friction

My judgement: the combat suits anyone who liked God Hand. Attacks are telegraphed, the dodge rewards nerve, and the grading rewards clean play. Edge said Hard, which unlocks once you finish Normal, is where the game goes "from the great to the legendary".

The friction sits outside the fighting. Eurogamer found the cutscenes "bite-size, snappy", but IGN disliked how often the game uses "filmstrip" scenes made of still models. Game Informer said the late-game vehicle and turret sections run "too long for how poorly they control". Critics complained about quick-time events, and some kill you outright: in Route 666 you survive only by pressing Jump on cue, and only Very Easy removes those prompts. Nintendo Life puts a first run at about 11 hours. Next to God Hand's lean structure, Bayonetta front-loads more story. Likely verdict: an excellent fit for the combat, mixed for the pacing.

## Editions and how to play it today

The 360 version is the original. Platinum's Tatsuya Minami, quoted by Digital Foundry, said Platinum built it first and then gave Sega the data to port to PS3. Nex Entertainment lists Bayonetta among its works and is credited with the port. Digital Foundry found the PS3 version running at 30–60% of the 360's frame rate in heavy fights, with loading stalls on item pickups and pauses. Patch 1.01 (28 January 2010) added an optional 4.5 GB install. Kotaku said it improved loading but not frame rate or textures.

Current official routes, none of them tested for this issue:

- Windows: on Steam since 11 April 2017. The store page promises 60fps at unlocked resolutions.
- Switch: released 16 February 2018 and published by Nintendo. Western physical copies of Bayonetta 2 come with a download code for it. Nintendo Life, citing Digital Foundry, says it holds 60fps more often than the Wii U port.
- PS4 and Xbox One: the Bayonetta & Vanquish 10th Anniversary Bundle, released 18 February 2020. Platinum says it runs at 4K and 60fps on PS4 Pro and Xbox One X.
- Original Xbox 360 and PS3 discs: Xbox 360 is the better of the two, as the reviews below explain. This issue makes no claim about running either through emulation.

## Reception at release

- Famitsu: 360 40/40 (10/10/10/10); PS3 38/40 (10/10/9/9), two reviewers docking a point for visuals. Reported by IGN's Anoop Gantayat, 21 October 2009.
- Eurogamer, Simon Parkin, 18 November 2009, reviewing the Japanese 360 release: 9/10, "a blast of creative brilliance".
- IGN, Ryan Clements, 22 December 2009: 360 9.5; PS3 8.2 ("a very disappointing piece of software"). IGN UK scored the 360 version 9.6.
- Game Informer, Joe Juba, 22 December 2009, 360: 9/10. "If you have the option, play it on Xbox 360."
- GameSpot, Lark Anderson, 4 January 2010: 360 9.0; PS3 8.0 (briefly published as 8.5 by mistake).
- Edge, issue 209, unsigned, 360 version tested, online 5 January 2010: 10/10.
- 1UP: 360 A; PS3 A−.
