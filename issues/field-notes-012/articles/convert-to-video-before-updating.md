Nintendo's manual for *Mario Kart 64* promises that the ghost will "drive exactly in the same manner as the stored data". The community decompilation of the game shows what that data is: not the kart's path but the controller, each entry a combination of buttons and stick position with a count of how many frames it was held. The manual's warnings line up with two limits in the code. "If you take too much time to reach the goal," the ghost may not be saved: the recording has room for 4,096 entries, and running out makes the whole ghost unsaveable. And if you "make severe driving mistakes (becoming trapped, leaving the course, etc.)", the recording is abandoned once Lakitu has to pick the kart up. My reading is that a rescue moves the kart in a way no button press explains, so the record would stop making sense from that point. The decompilers' comment says only that the input file is too long, or the kart was "picked up by lakitu or Out of bounds".

Even with the same program, the world has to be reborn identical. *Mario Kart Wii* ghosts are also stored inputs, and the community that has taken them apart has documented the Wiggler Glitch, in which leftover state from a moving obstacle produces a collision that never happened in the original run. "Ghosts subjected to this glitch are still legitimate, but cannot play back correctly." The reverse engineer stebler drew the conclusion: "the only way to correctly replay any ghost is to reproduce the original conditions in which the run was set, that is live replay against the original ghost, if any."

## The exact program

How exact? Shawn Hargreaves, who worked on MotoGP before moving to Microsoft, wrote in 2009 that its input replays could not be shared between Xbox and PC, and that a replay saved from a debug build of the game "would not work in release builds". The same game, compiled two ways, was two programs.

Live games meet this every patch day. Nintendo's update notes for *Super Smash Bros. Ultimate* record when old replays stop playing: version 3.0.1 kept "replay compatibility with Ver. 3.0.0, but not with Ver. 2.0.2 and earlier." Before version 4.0.0, Nintendo of Europe told players to "convert them by going to Vault -> Replays -> Replay Data -> Convert to Video before updating". Nintendo does not document the replay format. That a balance patch breaks old replays suggests they record commands rather than pictures, but that is my inference. The advice is the telling part: when the program is about to change, turn the instruction into a film.

## Two kinds of truth

That is the trade at the centre of this issue. A video keeps what a replay cannot: the clock, the screen, sometimes the hands. It loses what made the replay worth trusting. Change a single action in a Doom demo and, as Uwe Girlich wrote in 1996, "all the rest is garbage"; edit a video and it still looks like a video. An input replay is strong evidence that a run was possible in one program and weak evidence of anything else. Doom's demos have lasted three decades because players rebuilt the program around them, version byte by version byte. A replay without that care lasts as long as its program does, unless somebody converts it to video in time.
