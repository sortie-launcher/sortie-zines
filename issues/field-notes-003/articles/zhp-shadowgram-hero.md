## Why this one is the issue's deepest build

If this issue had to pick one game for players who love tinkering with a build, it would probably be *Z.H.P. Unlosing Ranger VS Darkdeath Evilman*, Nippon Ichi Software's 2010 PSP roguelike. Its Japanese title translates roughly as *Absolute Hero Modding Project*. It takes the Mystery Dungeon grid, resets you to level 1 every time you enter a dungeon, and then gives you three permanent progression systems that are as fiddly as a mech garage. It also has more anime-sitcom dialogue than anything else in this issue. Both statements are true, and the dialogue is the price of the systems.

RPGLand's 2010 review, quoted on Wikipedia, put it "somewhere comfortably between Shiren and Pokémon Mystery Dungeon". That's a useful reference point, because this issue also covers PMD.

## The loop and its three persistent systems

The premise is tokusatsu parody. The Absolute Victory Unlosing Ranger is hit by a car on the way to the final battle and hands his belt to a random passer-by, who is you. You promptly lose to the final boss and are sent off to train on Bizarro Earth. Each of the ten chapters ends with a rematch against Darkdeath Evilman, presented as a simplified, deliberately retro JRPG battle. Its graphics get more modern as you get stronger.

The real game takes place in turn-based Mystery Dungeon floors, viewed from an isometric angle with a rotatable camera. Enemies show their sight range on the ground at their feet, and when one dies its death cry can alert any enemy whose sight overlaps. Energy is your hunger: while you have energy you regenerate HP, and when it runs out you lose HP every turn. RPGFan notes that story dungeons are five or ten floors deep, while the optional ones go much deeper.

Here's what persists:

- **Total Level.** Your level resets to 1 every time you go back to base. When you clear a dungeon *or die*, the levels you gained are added to a permanent Total Level, and that raises your base stats. According to xarugas03's GameFAQs guide, the bonuses come at fixed intervals that grow wider as your Total Level rises. Even a failed run makes you stronger.
- **Body parts.** Equipment isn't just armour. You fill Head, Arm, Leg and Expansion slots with parts that show up on your sprite, such as snake hair, a robot arm or a bat wing. Bonuses are percentages, not flat values. Every piece wears down: most weapons lose about 3% condition per hit, and at 0% their skills switch off until you repair them at base.
- **The Shadowgram.** Once you unlock the Dark Clinic in Level-2, any piece of equipment can be broken down into a stat chip and placed on a human-shaped grid. A 1★ item gives +1 and a 6★ item gives +6. Booster devices sit on top of the chips. Eight heart-shaped Hero Energy sources send a flow along the chips, and an equipment Plug gets a percentage bonus equal to the number of tiles the flow has travelled. Twelve tiles give 12%. Routing that flow is a puzzle you'll keep rearranging for the rest of the game, and Total Level unlocks more grid space as you go. The guide puts the whole grid at 185 squares by roughly Total Level 2700.

At home base you choose six facilities, all of them unlocked by playing. Notable ones are the Caravan (a mid-dungeon stop where you can shop and save), the Insurer and the Blacksmith. Your Prinny wife sends energy-restoring lunches, as RPGamer explains.

## What failure costs

Death costs everything you were carrying: equipment, items and all your WP, the game's currency. You keep the experience, which goes into your Total Level. The guide adds a warning that matters if you play in short bursts: **leaving a dungeon in any other way also counts as a death.** That includes choosing Give Up, quitting from the PSP HOME menu, or the console powering off, and in those cases insurance doesn't save anything. To stop mid-run, reach a Caravan stop, save there, and quit before you take the next stairs. The game autosaves the moment you set off again.

There's some mercy built in. The Insurer facility unlocks after ten deaths and protects items in exchange for a premium. Your five equipped pieces are covered first, and at facility level 9 nine items are covered for free. A GameFAQs board veteran's beginner tips mention that dying also gives you an Unlosing Cape and that the Mod Shop sells a Plug-S. Both are community advice, not manual text.

The overall pattern: a short story dungeon is low risk, and an optional dungeon with a late exit point is high risk. RPGFan praised the choice you make when setting that exit. Set it too early and you miss loot. Set it too late and you're sprinting for the exit with no energy.

## The first session

The game opens with a long run of jokes: the car accident, the guaranteed loss to the final boss, the move into space, a sarcastic instructor called Etranger. Then come mandatory tutorials. RPGFan says the Blacksmith unlocks when you finish them. NISA's pre-release PlayStation Blog post quotes the in-game line "dying is a part of the game", and that's the attitude to take into your first dungeon.

## A first-session plan

- **Get through the opening and tutorials in one sitting.** Split across short sessions, they eat each one.
- **Treat your first deaths as investment.** Those levels still go into your Total Level, and the tenth death unlocks the Insurer.
- **Don't dual-wield yet.** Two weapons attack twice but burn energy and condition fast, which is why the guide saves it for tough enemies.
- **As soon as the Shadowgram opens, plan the flow, not just the chips.** The farther a Plug sits from its Hero Energy source, the bigger its bonus.
- **Know where your Caravan stops are before you descend.** That's the only safe way to put a run down.

## Who it suits, and what it costs

**Fit.** Piling up permanent mods, playing a number-routing puzzle on a body diagram, and attaching found parts to your hero makes this the closest thing in a roguelike to Armored Core's garage. Runs are short, and RPGamer pointed out that you can finish the story without endless grinding. The optional dungeons are where the depth is.

**Friction.**

- **Tone.** It's NIS anime comedy from start to finish. RPGFan found the end-of-chapter skits regressing to prologue-level maturity. RPGamer flagged two characters as unfortunately crass: a stereotyped Chinese fortune teller and a prince voiced as a gay caricature. Some of the insults wear thin. If this bothers you, expect it to bother you all the way through.
- **Boss spikes.** Four of RPGFan's fewer than ten deaths came from bosses harder than their dungeons.
- **Opaque depth.** RPGFan said the tutorials cover the basics and you'll need an FAQ for the hardcore systems. A few dungeon gadgets, like the RC car controller, aren't explained at all.
- **Repetition.** RPGamer's conclusion was that it always comes back to killing enemies and finding the stairs for one more floor.

## Controls and emulation: what's documented

The guide warns against PSP sleep mode mid-dungeon because an accidental power-off counts as a death. On an emulator, closing or suspending the app mid-run carries a similar risk; Field Notes did not play-test the game on any emulator or hardware for this issue. Emulator save states can bypass the game's death rules. Whether to use them is up to you, but they remove the stakes the design depends on.

## How it was received

Release-era reviews were consistently warm. Metacritic lists a Metascore of 81 from 19 critics, 18 of them positive. Worth Playing scored it 85 (27 October 2010) and called it one of NISA's best titles to date. The A.V. Club gave 83 (25 October 2010), crediting its trap-packed dungeon variety and its silliness. Gaming Nexus and GameShark both gave 83, and PSX Extreme gave 81. Wikipedia quotes GamePro's 4/5 review (12 November 2010), which called it a great introduction to roguelikes that still won't suit players who hate permanent penalties. RPGamer's review by Zach Welhouse (27 October 2010) praised its pile of optional subsystems. Its numerical score wasn't captured for this issue.

Later coverage: RPGFan's John Tucker reviewed it in July 2011, nine months after release, giving it 85 according to Metacritic. NIS president Sohei Niikawa later said the game shaped *The Guided Fate Paradox*. The 2022 Switch and PC re-releases are a different edition from the PSP original and aren't covered here.
