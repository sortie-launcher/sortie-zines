## Why a golf game is here

Neo Turf Masters is a golf game, and if golf is a walk you would rather skip, that sounds like the end of it. The claim here is narrower: Nazca's 1996 MVS cartridge, Big Tournament Golf in Japan, is a short planning puzzle with readable ball physics and an arcade economy that pays for risk. Nazca made Metal Slug the same year; Wikipedia reports its programmers were ex-Irem staff who had worked on Major Title 2.

Golf in one paragraph: every hole has a par, the strokes a good player should need (3, 4 or 5). Fewer is better. One under par is a birdie, two under an eagle, one over a bogey. Your score is your total against par.

## One shot, six decisions

The cabinet card is the whole manual. Lever up/down selects a club, left/right chooses direction, A is "shot/pat", B and C are "hook&slice", and "D is an unused button". StrategyWiki lists B as hook, C as slice.

![Neo Turf Masters MVS cabinet instruction card: lever selects club and direction, A shot/putt, B and C hook and slice, D unused. English-language card printed in Japan, © 1996 Nazca Corporation / SNK; scan hosted by StrategyWiki, credited there to neogeosoft.com](media/neo-turf-masters-wind-and-club-instruction-card.webp)

Each shot is a stack of choices made in seconds:

- **Club.** Each golfer carries 14; the lead image shows a 100-yard sand wedge.
- **Aim.** Mizuumi's competitive guide calls the minimap a loose representation, unsafe for precise approaches; its players aim with the curve arrow.
- **Curve.** Tap B or C first. The ball bends for its whole flight, so high shots curve more.
- **Power.** Press A, press again. Power scales with the club: 75% of a 100-yard club goes about 75 yards. Just under 100% is a penalty buffer; above it sits a Max shot, around 110% for most golfers, with a window of a few frames.
- **Height.** A second meter sweeps LOW, NICE, HIGH. Anything but Nice costs yardage. High lands softer but rides the wind longer; low rolls further and resists it. Red zones miss.
- **Spin.** Hidden: hold up or down while setting height for topspin or backspin, irons and wedges only. The in-game tutorial never mentions it.

## What the course does back

Wind runs 0–15 in sixteen directions, drawn relative to the minimap, not your view. Lie changes the arithmetic: rough trims power, heavy rough removes woods for everyone but the Veteran, four depths of sand progressively strangle distance, and water leaves most golfers half power. Greens have grain and slopes; the putting screen shows a grain readout, but IGN noted in 2008 that there are no detailed contour maps.

That is the case for this issue: wind and lie are inputs you read before swinging, and the competitive guides treat their effects as learnable, not luck.

## Six golfers, six meters

Giant Bomb explains the stats: Driving sets club distance (Adams's 1-wood 250 yards, Almeida's 280), Accuracy sets meter speed, Skill sets height-meter size, Recovery shrinks miss zones that grow in rough, and Putting splits the putting meter finer. SNK's 2008 Virtual Console page rates power, control, technique, recovery and putting:

- George Spinner, Young Hero: 3, 3, 3, 3, 3
- Thomas Stewart, Technician: 2, 4, 5, 4, 3
- Frank Adams, Veteran: 1, 2, 4, 5, 2
- Robert Landolt, Shot Maker: 4, 5, 2, 2, 4
- Fernando Almeida, Power Golfer: 5, 1, 1, 1, 1
- Toyoshige Takeno, Putt Master: 3, 2, 4, 2, 5

It is a loadout choice. Mizuumi says Almeida's ~320-yard drives reach almost every par 5 in two, but he can use his driver only from the tee and putts worst of all. Adams barely notices sand or rough.

## Holes as currency

In the arcade your credit is measured in holes. Dan Elektro's fan review and Giant Bomb say one coin buys three holes; Giant Bomb adds that operators can change that. Under-par scores buy extra holes, while over-par scores shorten the run. Elektro: "If you can't cut it, you're cut off." SNK's later Wii Virtual Console page supplies a precise hole-cost bonus table, but those home-version numbers aren't a verified MVS payout schedule here. The practical point survives: good play buys more game.

Mizuumi says the MVS release has a shot clock that AES, Neo Geo CD and Wii versions lack, yet IGN's Wii review describes countdown clocks. That conflict is unresolved. If Mizuumi is right, an emulator's MVS or AES system setting would decide whether you see the clock; that is an inference, not checked.

The cartridge has four 18-hole courses: Fujiyama Oriental (Japan), Grand Canyon (USA), Blue Lagoon (Australia) and Baden (Germany). The Neo Geo CD's hidden Scotland course and extra modes are not on the cartridge. Mizuumi documents a crash on Germany's 10th hole if the ball passes a particular patch near the front of the green; the wiki reports this in all versions of the arcade-derived game, not the separate NGPC design.

## Your first 20 minutes

- Start Stroke Play solo and watch the animated how-to-play after the credit begins; it is the tutorial.
- Pick Spinner: all 3s, no exceptions to learn.
- Choose Japan, which Mizuumi calls a course of short distances; hole 1 is a 426-yard par 4 with no hills.
- Off the tee, keep the suggested club, aim mid-fairway, chase 100% and NICE. Skip curve.
- Check wind every shot: more club into a headwind, aim upwind in a crosswind.
- Approach HIGH with a short iron so the ball stops.
- By hole four, try backspin: hold down while setting height.
- Leave putts short rather than long.
- Finish by starting a fresh run with Almeida to feel faster meters.

## Fit and friction

**Fit:** no menus, real decisions every shot, sessions as short as three holes. Versus is where it still lives: Match Play is two-player, and Mizuumi documents current tournaments on hardware and online.

**Friction:** it is still golf. No progression beyond your score on the same 72 holes; solo means chasing your own numbers. Putting information is thin, the minimap imprecise, spin hidden. In emulation a credit is one button press, which kills the economy's sting unless you hold yourself to one. Nothing here was playtested, including button mapping.

## The Neo Geo Pocket Color version

The 1999 handheld game is a separate design; Gamesdatabase credits Saurus as developer and SNK as publisher. It is cartoonish, with three courses (Japan, USA, Germany; no Australia) and the same six golfers' star ratings plus a hook/slice level. Per the English manual, the stick aims and changes club; on the Address screen left/right shifts stance to hook or slice, since there are no curve buttons. A starts and stops power (MAX is best), then a Spin Gauge sets trajectory: stop it high for a low shot, low for a high one, red ruins it. Holding down as you stop it gives backspin (irons from fairway or tee); up gives a running ball. B cancels, B plus stick scrolls the map, Option pauses.

![Neo Geo Pocket Color Neo Turf Masters, Address screen: power gauge to MAX, L/H spin gauge, course map, 1W-260Y; handheld edition, not the arcade game; region not identifiable from the frame; © SNK; screenshot via ScreenScraper, 4x point-scaled](media/neo-turf-masters-wind-and-club-ngpc.webp)

It adds a campaign. Crown runs three tournaments of preliminary plus final: place 16th or better, then 12th, then 8th in the preliminaries and win each final, or the run ends. The manual says you can save after each cleared match; Handicap rates you from 36; difficulty, stats and collection save automatically. Versus needs two consoles, two cartridges and a link cable, and awards single-use "Clubs of Legends". Link play under emulation was not tested.

## Reception, then and later

Release-era evidence is thin. Game Machine's 15 March 1996 chart placed the arcade game 12th among video-game software, a new entry rated 6.14: an operator popularity survey, not a review. I found no verified 1996 review scores; Famitsu, GameFan and Neo Geo Freak remain unchecked. On GameSpot in April 2000, Jeff Gerstmann gave the handheld 7.2, calling it "great fun" but faulting the link cable needed for two players.

Dan Elektro's undated neo-geo.com fan review of the MVS cartridge, archived by 2002, gave 10/10. Lucas M. Thomas gave the 2008 Wii release 7.5 on IGN, calling it "golf on steroids". Hamster's 2017 Arcade Archives release became Big Tournament Golf in 2019, per Giant Bomb.
