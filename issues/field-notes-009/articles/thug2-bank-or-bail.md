## A score engine dressed as a sport

Tony Hawk's Underground 2 is easy to dismiss as a skateboarding game, and as skateboarding, fair. As something you play, it is a score-attack game with a risk meter. Every trick adds base points and raises a multiplier, the total counts only when you land, and a bail wipes the string. The US manual adds a routing rule: outside free skate, "every time you repeat a trick, the trick's point value decreases," so you must "think about your 'line'."

This is not an emergent simulation. What THUG2 offers is a readable physics toy where each extra second of combo pays more and risks more.

## The combo is the loop

A combo ends when you roll on plain ground or fall. Grinds, lip tricks and manuals each raise a balance meter, the arc above the skater in the lead image, and holding it gets harder the longer you stay. A revert links a ramp landing; the manual's tip is to "revert then manual" off a quarterpipe. Gaps, the blue text in the string, add bonus links.

THUG2 adds tools. The **Sticker Slap** kicks you off a wall in the opposite direction; GameSpot's Jeff Gerstmann called it the most important addition, because it sends you back onto a rail you just left. The **Natas Spin** spins you on hydrants and posts. **Focus** is slow motion fuelled by a full special meter, limited to 15 seconds and three uses per combo. You can also step off the board mid-combo "for a limited time" to sprint to the next line. After a bail, a **Freak Out** meter lets you mash for bonus base score; Gerstmann thought the lost seconds rarely worth it.

So the recurring choice is: land now and bank a modest score, or take one more rail while the meter drifts. A 2004 fan guide adds that a clean landing adds 1,000 points and a sloppy one costs 500.

## The PS2 controls, from the manual

All of this comes from the US manual; nothing below has been playtested.

- X: ollie (press and release), hold to crouch. Square: flip tricks. Circle: grabs. Triangle: grinds, lip tricks, wallrides.
- Up then Down: manual. Down then Up: nose manual. Up and Down balance it; Left and Right balance grinds and lips.
- R2 or L2: revert on landing, spine transfer on take-off. R2 alone: switch stance. L2: nollie and pressure-flip positions.
- X straight into a wall: Sticker Slap. Up behind a vehicle: skitch.
- L1 and R1: faster spins. Both together: get off the board. L3: Focus.
- Two fan guides give the Natas Spin as Triangle plus R1 on a small post; the manual doesn't list it.

![US PS2 manual (NTSC U/C), pages 6–7: Sticker Slap on X, spine transfers on R2/L2, skitching, flatland, Focus Mode on L3 with its 15-second and three-per-combo limits, and the tantrum meter. © Activision; scan via Internet Archive](media/thug2-bank-or-bail-manual-controls.webp)

Focus sits on L3, a stick click; on a pad without clickable sticks, map it before you start.

## Start with Classic, not the tour

Classic is the route for someone allergic to padding. The manual promises the old goal lists, SKATE letters and high scores included, across the game's levels "plus 6 other re-released levels", with 10 goals per level, a two-minute limit and branching progression. Wikipedia names the six: School and Downhill Jam (first game), Philadelphia (second), Canada, Los Angeles and Airport (third).

A long GameFAQs walkthrough supplies the numbers. Normal needs six goals to advance, Sick eight. Stat points scattered through levels count for every skater. Barcelona, the first level, asks for 30,000, 60,000 and 100,000 points plus a 10,000-point combo on Normal. Eurogamer's Tom Bramwell found Classic "rock hard at times" next to Story; IGN noted you can switch off manuals, reverts, walking and spin taps.

The appeal is the unit of failure: a bad run costs two minutes, and the next goal is printed on a list.

## Story mode and the Jackass problem

Story is the World Destruction Tour: Team Hawk against Team Bam Margera, city to city. You start each level as your created skater with a pro partner; finding a hidden guest and a secret character unlocks more goals. Goal points gate progress, goals have no on-screen markers, and details sit in a pause-menu list. GameSpot estimated five to seven hours.

The tone is pranks, property damage and crude humour. Bramwell called it "a feature-length episode of Jackass". IGN's Douglass C. Perry complained that many goals, such as throwing apples or ramming bulls, are "not really skateboarding goals", and he called the walking and climbing "clunky, awkward". The catch for a Classic-first player is that the manual says you earn extra special-trick slots beyond the starting four in Story.

## Your first 20 minutes

- Minutes 0–5: choose High Score/Free Skate in Boston, which the manual says is open from the start. Use only three things: ollie on X, Triangle near a rail, then Up-Down as you land. Try to land three tricks in one string.
- Minutes 5–10: find a quarterpipe. Go up, grab with Circle, press R2 as you come back down, then go straight into Up-Down. That revert-to-manual link is the whole game in miniature.
- Minutes 10–20: Classic, Normal, Barcelona. Spend the first run reading the goal list in the pause menu, then go after one goal per run. When you bail, let the Freak Out meter pass.
- If the inputs still won't stick, Story mode's Training level hands out tutorial goals one move at a time. Eurogamer calls the tutorial "necessarily extensive".

## The editors and what's left of multiplayer

The editors are the tinkering hook, though they build content rather than tune physics. Create-A-Park lets you place pieces, raise ground, draw freeform rails and define gaps with your own names, scores and type limits; a memory meter caps it, and allowing fewer players frees room. Create-A-Goal holds ten goals per level, 120 per save. Create-A-Trick sequences trimmed animations on a timeline, one holdable grab per trick. Create-A-Graphic stacks ten layers for tags and decks.

![Create-A-Graphic editor with ten layers and colour sliders; the triangle and X prompts indicate a PlayStation version. GameSpot review gallery image whose filename is dated 2004-07-19, so it may show pre-release code. © Activision; GameSpot watermark](media/thug2-bank-or-bail-create-a-graphic.webp)

Offline, two controllers give split screen: Trick Attack, Combo Mambo, Slap!, Graffiti, H.O.R.S.E., Scavenger Hunt, Firefight and more. Treat official online, including Elimiskate and Goal Attack, as gone: a Tony Hawk fan wiki says Neversoft pulled THUG2's server files in 2007, and GameSpy closed in 2014.

## Fit and friction

It fits through short runs, explicit scoring and a fighting-game appeal: a long combo is a drillable skill. Against that, Bramwell warned of "a pretty steep learning curve for the first few hours". Classic still means collecting letters and a secret tape. Story adds pedestrian errands and humour you may skip. On PS2 hardware, Gerstmann reported a "pretty sketchy frame rate" and widescreen blur.

The upstream PCSX2 desktop GameIndex rates SLUS-20965, Redump's USA disc, at compatibility 5, with several fixes including a VU1 rounding setting marked "Crashes without." That is upstream data, not a tested result.

## Reception in 2004 and after

GameSpot's 2009 PS2 page showed Gerstmann's score as 8.3; today's page for the same review displays 8.2. IGN gave 8.6, Eurogamer 8/10 for PS2, and Game Informer 9.75. Metacritic lists 83 for PS2 from 59 critics. The moves drew praise, the story shrugs, and Classic kept getting singled out.

Later reassessment is thin for the PS2 release. Its main legacy is the PC version: THUG Pro, a fan mod on Underground 2's engine that has been updated since 2013. Reviewers of 2015's Pro Skater 5 pointed players to it instead.
